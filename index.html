Who we are

Traditional donations often disappear into a black box. Large sums leave the donor’s hands, yet almost no one can see exactly how, when, or whether those funds were used for their intended purpose. Annual reports arrive later. By then the trail is already gone.

We built VeriChar because accountability should not be an afterthought. It should be written into the ledger itself.

Large organizations sometimes treat philanthropy as a public-relations exercise. Conditional escrows change that. The platform does not ask anyone to trust a promise. It locks the funds until the charity hits a verified milestone. Both sides are protected: the organization only releases money when the work is done, and the charity can show clear, public proof that it was done.

Too much donated money is lost to intermediary fees, administration, and compliance overhead. By removing the middleman custodian entirely, more of every dollar can reach the ground.

Brianne Salato founded and architects VeriChar. She had no background in finance, crypto, XRPL, geopolitics, or software. She kept following the same pattern until the truth was unavoidable: the problem was not generosity. The problem was the exploitation of it. The need was as real as the charities it serves. She built this because the answer was clear—code must enforce integrity where trust has failed.

VeriChar is a hands-on, founder-led project. The software should make its rules visible and its limits clear. The public experience is a simulation, while Testnet work exercises the ledger flows. The project is being built transparently, one verifiable step at a time.

What we are building

VeriChar is verifiable charity on chain. We are creating infrastructure for transparent, audit-ready giving that can be verified on the public ledger.

Proof before payment. Funds are itemized, locked, and released only after the required invoice and delivery proof are validated.

Public accountability. The release path remains visible and auditable, not hidden behind opaque internal controls.

Why Ripple & XRPL

VeriChar has domain-linked accounts on XRPL Mainnet and Testnet. XRPL provides a public settlement record and native conditional escrow; the backend creates, finishes, and cancels XRP escrows on Testnet, with integration tests exercising those ledger flows.

The intended model gives each verified charity its own account and uses a separate escrow for each need, with the vendor fixed as the destination. Release uses a crypto-condition fulfillment after invoice and delivery evidence are reviewed. XRPL records the transaction and settlement trail for public audit; evidence review remains an operational responsibility.

This design makes fund movement and release conditions directly verifiable, reducing reliance on opaque internal records. Charity qualification and applicable legal and compliance requirements remain part of deployment.

Legal and compliance

Ledger controls support transparent records and accountable fund flows. Charity qualification, applicable law, and professional legal and tax review remain essential to deployment.

Business model

VeriChar Conditional Escrow. A verified 501(c)(3) gets its own XRPL account and a dedicated lock for each need. Donors and corporations can audit every release by record, not just by claim.

Free for donors, charities, and vendors. There is no settlement fee on the giving flow and no 1% vendor takerate.

Tax compliance remains with the charity. The ledger trail supports substantiation and donor records, but VeriChar does not issue deductions, file returns, or act as a donor-advised fund.

Enterprise SaaS. The same proof-backed trails can be packaged for audit support, compliance workflows, and reporting services.
